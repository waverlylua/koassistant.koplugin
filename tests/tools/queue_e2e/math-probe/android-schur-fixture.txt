Write the quadratic in block form as

$$f(x,y)=\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\B^T&C\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix}.$$

Here $A$ and $C$ are symmetric, and joint convexity of $f$ means precisely that its Hessian, up to the irrelevant factor \(2\), is positive semidefinite:

$$M=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\B^{T}&C\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0.$$

For a fixed $x$, the $y$-dependent part is

$$y^TCy+2x^TBy.$$

If $C$ is positive definite, setting its gradient with respect to $y$ to zero gives

$$2B^Tx+2Cy=0,\qquad y^\star=-C^{-1}B^Tx.$$

Substitution yields

$$g(x)=f(x,y^\star)=x^T\bigl(A-BC^{-1}B^T\bigr)x.$$

The matrix $A-BC^{-1}B^T$ is called the Schur complement of $C$ in the block matrix $M$.

The key convex-analysis principle is that partial minimization preserves convexity. Hence

$$A-BC^\dagger B^T\succeq0.$$

The generalized Schur-complement conditions use the pseudoinverse and range:

$$C\succeq0,\quad \operatorname{range}(B^T)\subseteq\operatorname{range}(C), \quad A-BC^\dagger B^T\succeq0.$$

$$\bigl(x\bigr)\quad\Bigl[x\Bigr]\quad\biggl\lfloor x\biggr\rfloor\quad\Biggl\lVert x\Biggr\rVert$$

$$A\preceq B\succeq C\prec D\succ E\quad F^{\dagger}\quad G^{\ddagger}$$
